Gynecomastia Surgery in Lucknow

Gynecomastia refers to enlargement of male breast glandular tissue. A medical consultation helps identify the underlying cause and discuss appropriate management, including surgical options where suitable.

Introduction

Gynecomastia is the enlargement of male breast glandular tissue. It may result from various causes including hormonal changes, certain medications, underlying medical conditions, or in some cases no specific cause is identified. It is distinct from pseudo-gynecomastia, which relates to fat deposition without glandular enlargement. Management depends on the underlying cause, the duration, the degree of enlargement and individual concerns. Where surgery is considered appropriate, it typically involves removal of the excess glandular tissue, sometimes combined with liposuction for surrounding fat.

Who May Be Suitable

Surgical management may be discussed for individuals who:

  • Have persistent glandular enlargement where underlying causes have been evaluated.
  • Have stable concerns that have not resolved with conservative management.
  • Are in good general health without conditions that increase surgical risk.
  • Have realistic expectations about outcomes.
  • Do not smoke, or are willing to stop smoking before and after the procedure.

Consultation and Assessment Process

Your consultation with Dr. Brajesh Pathak includes a review of your medical history, current medications, the duration of your concerns and any previous evaluations. An examination is conducted to assess whether the enlargement reflects glandular tissue, fat, or both. Dr. Pathak explains the possible causes, the role of any further evaluation, and the management options available — including whether surgery is appropriate in your case. If surgery is suitable, the approach, expected outcomes, recovery and risks are discussed.

Procedure Overview

Where gynecomastia surgery is undertaken, the procedure is performed under appropriate anaesthesia. The approach depends on the underlying composition — glandular tissue is typically removed through a small incision, often near the areola, while excess fat may be addressed with liposuction. In selected cases involving excess skin, additional skin removal may be discussed. Specific procedural details, anaesthesia and facility considerations are explained during your consultation.

Recovery Information

Following the procedure, swelling, bruising and mild discomfort are common and typically settle over the following weeks. A compression garment is usually recommended for a period to support healing. Detailed post-procedure care instructions are provided, including guidance on activity, wound care and follow-up appointments. Most individuals can resume routine activities within a week or two, with specific restrictions explained during the consultation. Recovery timelines vary between individuals.

Possible Risks and Limitations

As with any surgical procedure, gynecomastia surgery carries potential risks, which may include:

  • Bleeding, infection or delayed healing.
  • Adverse reaction to anaesthesia.
  • Asymmetry, irregular contours or under-correction.
  • Changes in nipple or skin sensation, which may be temporary or prolonged.
  • Visible scarring, though typically placed to be discreet.
  • Need for additional procedures in selected cases.
  • Recurrence if underlying cause persists — discussed during consultation.
