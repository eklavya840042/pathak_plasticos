Beard transplant overview

A beard transplant uses hair follicles taken from a donor area — typically the scalp — and places them into areas of the beard, moustache or cheeks where growth is patchy or absent. It is a facial hair restoration procedure, and the planning is different from scalp restoration because facial hair direction, angle and density are distinctive.

Results depend on the cause of the patchiness, the quality of donor hair, skin characteristics and how the grafts are planned. A consultation is needed to understand what is realistic for your face.

Who may be suitable

  • Patchy beard growth, scarring-related gaps, or areas where beard hair has never developed
  • Adequate donor hair density in the scalp or other donor areas
  • Realistic expectations about density, hair texture and growth direction
  • No active skin infection or inflammation in the recipient area
  • General health that allows a minor surgical procedure

If the underlying reason for patchy growth is a skin condition, a hormonal issue or another medical problem, that needs to be understood before a transplant is considered.

Consultation and assessment

During consultation, the surgeon reviews your facial hair pattern, how long the concern has been present, any scarring, your donor supply and your goals. Beard style, hairline design and the density that can be achieved are discussed honestly — facial hair transplants cannot create unlimited density, and texture after transplantation may differ from natural beard hair.

Procedure overview

Where suitable, follicles are harvested from the donor area and placed into small recipient sites in the beard region under local anaesthesia. The number of grafts, session length and technique are confirmed after assessment. The design of the beard line and the direction of graft placement are planned with you beforehand.

Recovery

  • Small scabs and redness in the treated area are common in the first week or two.
  • Shaving and grooming in the treated area are restricted for a period — your surgeon will tell you when to resume.
  • Transplanted hair often sheds before regrowth begins.
  • Growth is judged over months, and density develops gradually.

Possible risks and limitations

  • Temporary swelling, redness or tenderness
  • Small scars at donor or recipient sites
  • Uneven growth, or density lower than anticipated, sometimes requiring a further session
  • Hair direction or texture that differs from natural beard hair
  • Folliculitis or other skin irritation during healing

These risks are discussed in detail before any decision, along with what aftercare involves.
