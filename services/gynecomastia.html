Gynecomastia Surgery in Lucknow

Gynecomastia refers to enlargement of male breast glandular tissue. A medical consultation helps identify the underlying cause and discuss appropriate management, including surgical options where suitable.

What gynecomastia is

Gynecomastia is enlargement of the male breast. It can involve glandular tissue, fatty tissue, or both, and it is common — particularly during adolescence and in later life. Many cases are benign, but the cause matters because it changes what treatment is appropriate.

Possible contributors include hormonal changes, certain medicines, some medical conditions and weight gain. Occasionally, other breast conditions need to be excluded. This is why an assessment comes before any discussion of surgery.

Important: a new lump, nipple discharge, skin changes or pain on one side should be assessed promptly and is not treated as a cosmetic concern.

Who may be suitable for surgery

  • Enlargement that has been stable for a reasonable period and is not explained by an untreated medical cause
  • Discomfort, self-consciousness or a desire for a flatter chest contour
  • Good skin elasticity, or a plan that accounts for excess skin
  • General health that allows surgery, with a stable weight
  • Realistic expectations about contour, scarring and recovery

Consultation and assessment

The consultation includes a history of when the enlargement began, medicines and supplements, weight history and any related symptoms. Examination helps determine whether the tissue involved is mainly glandular or fatty, which influences the approach discussed.

Investigations may be suggested where the history or examination warrants them. If the cause is a medicine or another condition, addressing that may be the first step.

Procedure overview

Where surgery is appropriate, the approach depends on the tissue involved and the amount of skin excess. Options may include removal of glandular tissue, liposuction, or a combination, and in some cases skin excision. The technique, anaesthesia, expected scarring and recovery are confirmed with you before surgery.

Recovery

  • Swelling, bruising and tightness are expected initially.
  • A compression garment is typically worn for a set period.
  • Desk work is often possible within a short time; upper-body exercise is restricted for longer.
  • Final contour settles gradually over weeks and months.

Possible risks and limitations

  • Bleeding, infection, fluid collection or delayed healing
  • Scarring, which varies with technique and healing
  • Temporary or persistent changes in nipple or skin sensation
  • Contour irregularities, asymmetry or residual tissue
  • Need for revision surgery in some cases
